# Supplement review: what Kuro took, and how strong the evidence is

Between 2022 and his death in Oct 2026 Kuro was given more than 40 supplement products ([`prescriptions.csv`](../../data/processed/clinical/prescriptions.csv), [`invoice_items.csv`](../../data/processed/clinical/invoice_items.csv), the [DAP diet answers](../../data/dog_aging_project/dap_surveys_kuro.csv)). Brand-specific trials rarely exist, so this review groups the products by **active ingredient** and asks: for the reason Kuro was given it, how good is the evidence in **dogs**?

This is a desk review of published evidence, not veterinary advice. It judges the evidence, not the decisions made at the time.

## How the evidence is graded

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Clinically validated** | Randomised controlled trials in client-owned dogs with clinical outcomes, replicated or supported by a meta-analysis, **for the use Kuro had** |
| **Validated, but for another use** | Strong evidence exists, but for a condition Kuro wasn't shown to have |
| **Limited clinical evidence** | Some trials in dogs, but small, single, industry-funded, mixed, or measuring lab markers rather than how the dog does |
| **Research stage** | Laboratory, rodent or human studies, biomarker studies in healthy dogs, or uncontrolled pilots. Plausible, but unproven in sick dogs |
| **Tested and not supported** | Trials in dogs looked for the benefit and didn't find it |
| **No credible evidence** | No studies found, or no plausible mechanism |

**In short:**
- **Only one supplement group was clinically validated for its purpose:** omega-3s for joint pain. Kuro had a dedicated fish-oil product only in 2022, though his 2024 raw diet supplied a meaningful dose.
- **Two groups were tested in dogs and failed:** glucosamine/chondroitin for arthritis pain, and turkey tail for tumour survival.
- **B12 and digestive enzymes are proven only for specific deficiencies,** and Kuro was never tested for either.
- **Most of the rest is research-stage or has no credible evidence.**

## Notes by group

### Joints

- **Omega-3 (EPA + DHA): clinically validated.** Three randomised trials in 2010 fed dogs with osteoarthritis diets rich in fish oil. They improved weight-bearing in a force-plate study of 38 dogs, owner-rated function in a 127-dog study across 18 clinics, and lameness and weight-bearing at the highest dose in a dose-titration study. A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis of nutraceuticals for osteoarthritis pain concluded that omega-3s are the supplement with convincing evidence. The caveats: effects are modest, several outcomes were owner-rated, and the benefit depends on the dose of EPA + DHA, best delivered through a therapeutic diet or a measured fish-oil dose.
- **Green-lipped mussel: limited clinical evidence.** Several small randomised trials, mostly of the lipid extract PCSO-524 (Antinol), report better lameness and weight-bearing scores. But many were funded by the manufacturer and run by the same investigators, and a 2022 crossover trial of a mussel, curcumin and blackcurrant product found only partial benefit over placebo. It's reasonable to try, but not proven.
- **Glucosamine / chondroitin: tested and not supported.** The 2022 meta-analysis found no analgesic effect for canine osteoarthritis and recommended against prescribing them for pain.
- **Turmeric / curcumin: limited for joints.** The two randomised trials both tested combination products (curcumin with collagen and green tea; curcumin with mussel and blackcurrant), so curcumin's own effect isn't isolated. The results were modest or partial.

**For Kuro:** his joint supplements were the weaker options (glucosamine, mussel) rather than a measured omega-3 dose. His **prescribed analgesics** (Galliprant, gabapentin) and acupuncture carried most of the pain control in 2025–26.

### Liver

- **SAMe + silybin (Samylin): limited clinical evidence.**
  - The one randomised trial in dogs (Denamarin with the chemotherapy drug lomustine) found smaller rises in liver enzymes: 68% of dogs on the supplement versus 84% without it. That was prevention during chemotherapy, a different situation from Kuro's.
  - SAMe alone didn't prevent the liver changes caused by prednisolone in a controlled study. That's relevant, because Kuro was on prednisolone for long periods.
  - There are no randomised trials in dogs with established liver disease.
  - It's widely used, safe and plausible, but the benefit isn't proven.
- **Milk thistle and herbal liver tonics: research stage.** A 2013 review found no randomised trials of milk thistle in animals with liver disease; the dog data come from poisoning experiments. Blends like Adored Beast Liver Tonic have no product-level studies.
- **Curcumin for the liver: research stage.**

**For Kuro:** at times he had **three liver products at once** (Samylin, a liver tonic and Maximus Hep), and none has strong evidence. A single well-chosen product with a defined dose would have been easier to keep stocked; the archive records Samylin running out unnoticed.

### Gut and pancreas

- **Probiotics: limited clinical evidence.**
  - A 2019 systematic review of 17 trials found small, mostly biased studies.
  - In chronic gut disease, diet did the work and probiotics added no significant benefit.
  - A 2024 meta-analysis found nutraceuticals didn't shorten acute diarrhoea by a clinically meaningful amount.
  - Probiotics are low-risk and may help in stress- or antibiotic-related diarrhoea.
- **Digestive enzymes: validated, but for another use.** Enzyme replacement is the established treatment for **exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (EPI)**, where the pancreas stops producing enzymes. Kuro had **pancreatitis**, not diagnosed EPI. No enzyme test (TLI) is on record, and there's no good evidence that enzymes help pancreatitis or prevent flares.
- **Slippery elm and herbal gut blends: research stage / traditional use.** Activated charcoal is used for poisoning; there's no evidence for routine daily gut support.

### Tumour and immunity

- **Turkey tail (Coriolus versicolor PSP): tested and not supported.**
  - A 2012 pilot of 15 dogs with splenic haemangiosarcoma and no placebo group reported unusually long survival, and was widely publicised.
  - The **2022 randomised trial** (Gedney et al.) found that PSP **did not extend survival** compared with standard chemotherapy, and **females on PSP alone did worse**.
- **Other mushroom blends** (CAS Options, Vital Defense): no dog trials.

**For Kuro:** his tumour was never typed, so it isn't known whether it was a haemangiosarcoma. Even so, the best trial didn't support turkey tail for survival.

### Vitamins, minerals and nerves

- **Vitamin B12: validated, but for another use.** B12 reliably corrects **proven deficiency**. A 2018 randomised trial in dogs with chronic gut disease found oral tablets worked as well as injections. **There's no B12 test in Kuro's records**, so it's unknown whether he was deficient. There's no evidence B12 relieves nerve pain. Neuroforte's ingredients aren't on record.
- **Multivitamin / mineral: established nutrition practice.** A home-cooked diet needs a balancing supplement to meet nutrient requirements; that's standard veterinary nutrition rather than a trial question. The point is to **balance** the diet, not to add more: his 2024 recipe was already near the vitamin D limit and high in iron (see the [diet review](./diet-review.md)), so an extra multivitamin could push some nutrients over.

### Kidney

- **Aminavast: no credible evidence.** The manufacturer cites unpublished trials. No independent, peer-reviewed study of this product was found; the published trials of kidney supplements in dogs test different products. **Diet and monitoring** are the established care for kidney disease.

### Longevity

- **Resveratrol: research stage.** There are safety studies, biomarker changes in healthy dogs, and mixed cognition results. No study shows longer or healthier life in dogs.

### Homeopathy and cell salts

- **Homeopathy (Arnica, Hypericum, Strophanthus): no credible evidence.** Systematic reviews of veterinary homeopathy trials found few reliable trials and very limited evidence of any effect beyond placebo. At 30C the remedy is diluted so far that no molecules of the original substance remain. The remedies themselves are harmless; the risk is **delaying effective pain relief**.
- **Cell salts: no credible evidence.** No clinical studies in dogs were found.

### Chinese herbal formulas

- **Xiao Chai Hu Tang: research stage, with safety signals.**
  - In humans it's linked to **interstitial pneumonitis** and **liver injury**.
  - Guidance advises against it in liver disease and with **low platelets**. Kuro had a bleeding-prone tumour and was anaemic.
  - The evidence for benefit is mostly from animal models.
- **Si Miao San: research stage.** The evidence is mouse studies of gout and a human review protocol. No dog trials were found.
- **Product quality:** herbal products vary in content and can be contaminated, and none of these formulas has been trialled in dogs.

## Safety points relevant to Kuro

- **Bleeding risk (theoretical, unstudied):**
  - Curcumin, fish oil and green-lipped mussel all have mild anti-clotting effects in other contexts.
  - Kuro had a tumour that bled into his abdomen in 2024 and had recurrent anaemia.
  - No study tests these supplements in dogs with bleeding tumours, so this is a caution, not evidence of harm.
  - It's the kind of combination worth listing for the vet.
- **Many products at once:** the July 2024 and June 2025 care lists each have about seven supplements given at the same time, alongside prescription medicines (prednisolone, and later Galliprant and gabapentin).
  - No single list was kept. The archive records supplements running out unnoticed, and products given without anyone reviewing the combination.
  - Fewer products, each chosen for evidence, would have been simpler to give, stock and review.

## What the evidence would have supported

| Need | Best-supported option | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Arthritis pain | Prescribed analgesics, plus omega-3 at a measured EPA + DHA dose (or a therapeutic joint diet) | Validated |
| Second-line for joints | Green-lipped mussel extract | Limited |
| Liver enzymes | SAMe + silybin, after ruling out causes (including prednisolone) | Limited |
| Gut upsets | Diet first; a probiotic is low-risk | Limited |
| Home-cooked diet | One balancing supplement, matched to the recipe | Established practice |
| Suspected B12 or enzyme deficiency | **Test first** (serum cobalamin, TLI), then supplement if low | Validated for confirmed deficiency |
