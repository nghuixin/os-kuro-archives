# Kuro against Dog Aging Project findings

How Kuro's [DAP survey answers](./dap-surveys.md) and records compare with what Dog Aging Project (DAP) studies report for dogs like him. For each finding, the closest comparison group is used where the paper gives one: age, size, sex, breed type.

**Kuro:**
- male, neutered, mixed breed (Embark: 52% Golden Retriever, 34% Japanese Spitz);
- 28 kg at age 12, about 20 kg (43 lb) in his last year;
- died at **16.6 years**, at home and unassisted, with an untyped splenic tumour (cause in the [health summary](../case-study/health-summary.md)).

> **Read this as a profile, not a statistic.** DAP studies are of US dogs whose owners volunteered, and the data are owner-reported. Kuro lived in Malaysia, and his answers were given after his death. One dog can't be "significant" against a cohort. What this shows is where he sits relative to published averages, and which findings his case fits or doesn't.

## Lifespan

**Source:** Chiu et al., *GeroScience* 2026. 41,047 DAP dogs, 11,739 deaths.

- **Size:** median lifespan falls steadily with size, from about **13–14 years** in toy and small breeds to **9–10 years** in giants. Within a size class, the middle half of dogs span about 4 years.
- **Breed type:** within the same size and sex, recognised purebreds lived shorter lives than mixed breeds: **0.7–1.6 years shorter in males**.
- **Neutering:** made no significant difference (89% of dogs were neutered).
- **Factors linked to lower mortality** (observational):
  - good owner-rated health;
  - physical activity, and being energetic or playful;
  - visiting parks;
  - **eating grass**.
- **Factors linked to higher mortality:**
  - chronic conditions;
  - **eating less than usual**;
  - urinating or defecating frequently when left alone.

**Kuro:**
- **Lifespan:** at 16.6 years he lived past the median for even the smallest dogs, despite being medium-to-large. That likely puts him in the top quarter for his size, though the paper's per-size table (needed to say exactly) wasn't available.
- **Profile:** his traits line up with the longer-lived profile. He was a mixed-breed male, playful (an hour a day with his Kong balls), occasionally ate grass, and had a **voracious appetite** to the end.
- **Late warning sign:** the higher-mortality signal of **house soiling** appeared only in his last year.
- **Limits:** these are associations from survey data, not causes.

## Multimorbidity

**Sources:**
- Fang et al., *PLoS Comput Biol* 2025: 26,614 dogs.
- Hoffman, *Innovation in Aging* 2025: abstract, more than 50,000 dogs.

**Findings:**
- **71.6%** of DAP dogs had **three or more** conditions, at a median age of 7.75.
- The more conditions accumulate, the higher the risk of death.
- **Dogs with osteoarthritis at baseline** went on to more diagnoses and more multimorbidity.
- **Specific combinations** predicted death better than single conditions.
- **Cataracts** clustered with blindness.
- **Anaemia** was linked through protein in the urine to chronic kidney disease.

**Kuro's conditions in his last 2.5 years:**
1. splenic tumour;
2. recurrent anaemia;
3. pancreatitis;
4. arthritis and nerve pain;
5. sarcopenia (muscle loss);
6. cataract and a cyst above the eye;
7. recurrent skin wounds;

plus rising urea in 2025–26.

**How he fits:**
- **Count:** at least seven conditions, which is expected for a dog more than twice the cohort's median age.
- **Order:** joint signs appeared first (stiffness in 2024, arthritis diagnosed in 2025), and other conditions accumulated after. That matches the osteoarthritis-first path in the 2025 abstract.
- **Anaemia:** his came from bleeding (the tumour), not kidney disease, so it isn't part of the kidney cluster.
- **Gaps in the papers:** the network paper reports no cancer pairs and doesn't cover pancreatitis, so those parts of his history can't be compared.

## Disease history by size and age

**Source:** Nam et al., *PLoS ONE* 2024. 27,541 dogs; lifetime prevalence by owner report.

| Category | DAP seniors (11+) | Kuro |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer / tumour | 15% | Yes (splenic tumour, 2024; type never determined, not shown to be malignant) |
| Eye | 28% | Yes (cataract) |
| Gastrointestinal | 18% | Mild: occasional diarrhoea (2023, Feb 2025); logged stools mostly ideal (Purina 2–3 of 7) |
| Liver / pancreas | 8% | Yes (pancreatitis 2024 and 2025; mild liver enzyme rises) |
| Neurologic | 12% | Possibly ("nerve pain", 2025) |
| Kidney / urinary | 15% | Borderline (urea rising; creatinine and SDMA normal) |
| Orthopaedic | 19% across all ages, rising steeply with age and size | Yes |

- **Cancer** prevalence rose about **2.5-fold for every 4 years of age** at a given size, more steeply in larger dogs.
- **Orthopaedic disease** roughly doubled per 4 years of age, also steeper in large dogs.

**How he fits:**
- **Overall:** he had clear disease in five of the seven categories (cancer/tumour, eye, liver/pancreas, orthopaedic, and nerve pain under neurologic), against senior averages of 8–28% each. Gut and kidney were borderline.
- **Why that's expected:** the "senior" group starts at 11 and most of it is far younger than 16, so prevalence at his age would be much higher than these figures.
- **Liver/pancreas** is the least common category (8%) and the most distinctive part of his history.
- **His tumour at 14** falls in this category, which rises steeply with age in larger dogs. It counts as a tumour, not a confirmed cancer: it was never typed, he lived more than two years with it, and the spread reported in 2024 wasn't found at post mortem.

## Cognition

**Source:** Yarborough et al., *Scientific Reports* 2022. 15,019 dogs, scored with the same cognition survey Kuro was answered with.

**Findings:**
- **1.4%** met the cognitive dysfunction cut-off.
- In dogs over 10, the odds rose **52% per year** of age, after adjusting for health, activity, breed and neutering.
- **Inactive dogs had 6.47 times the odds** of very active dogs.
- A history of eye or ear disorders was also linked to dysfunction.

**How he fits:**
- **Score:** 36, well under the cut-off of 50, and under the borderline range of 40–49. Six years past 10, his age alone would predict substantially raised odds.
- **Two of his points don't reflect cognition:** his difficulty finding dropped food is likely his cataracts, and the extra house soiling is likely mobility.
- **Activity:** he stayed moderately active, which is consistent with the activity finding. The study can't say whether activity protects cognition or cognition sustains activity.

## End of life

**Sources:**
- Pearson et al., *JAVMA* 2024: 2,570 deaths.
- McNulty et al., *JAVMA* 2026: 646 deaths, mean age 13.0.

| Measure | DAP | Kuro |
|---|---|---|
| Euthanised | 85.4% / 83.0% | No |
| Died at home with no vet involved | 14.7% | Yes |
| Primary cause (owner view) | Illness 58% (cancer 29.7%), old age 29–31% | Illness (splenic tumour, DAP's "cancer or tumors" category; not shown to be malignant) |
| Old age as cause in dogs over 15 | 54.9% | No |
| Old-age signs, last 2 weeks | Euthanised 5.08; unassisted 3.62 | 5 |
| Most common old-age signs | Less active 55%; weakness 54%; eating/drinking less 43%; pain/stiffness 40% | Weakness and pain yes; less active and eating less, no |
| Quality of life, last 2 weeks (dogs over 15) | Favourable 44.4%, unfavourable 42.1% | 6 of 7, favourable |
| Owner expected death within a month | 49.6% | No |
| Primary reason for euthanasia, among euthanised dogs | Pain/suffering 48.5%; poor quality of life 24.8% | Not applicable |

**What stands out:**
- **His death was atypical** in manner, place and expectation. Most DAP dogs, especially those over 15, were euthanised after a decline their owners could see coming.
- **His signs matched the euthanised profile:** five old-age signs in his last two weeks, the same as the euthanised average. Weakness and pain were the two signs most strongly tied to euthanasia in the DAP data.
- **But two common decline signs were absent:** reduced activity and reduced appetite. Along with his favourable quality-of-life rating, that may explain why euthanasia wasn't being considered.
- **Cause of death:** owners of dogs over 15 most often cited "old age". Kuro's family cited his tumour, and the post-mortem added sudden death into the garden pond, which the DAP categories capture only loosely.

## What this adds up to

- **By DAP standards, Kuro was a long-lived dog** who accumulated an expected, heavy load of age-related disease.
- **His cognition and appetite held up** better than his age would predict.
- **His decline was mainly physical:** weakness and pain, consistent with his [CBPI](../../README.md#reading-the-pain-scores) and [HHHHHMM](../../README.md#reading-the-quality-of-life-scores) scores.
- **His death broke from the typical DAP pattern:** sudden, unassisted and unexpected, in a dog whose symptom profile matched those who are usually euthanised.

## Limits

- **Owner-reported and retrospective.** DAP data are owner-reported. Kuro's answers were given after his death and checked against records ([dap-surveys.md](./dap-surveys.md)).
- **Different populations.** DAP dogs are US volunteers; Kuro lived in Malaysia.
- **Figures not always available for his exact subgroup.** Some figures (median lifespan for his size and sex, multimorbidity by age, disease prevalence at 16) are in paper tables that weren't accessible here. Where that's the case, the comparison uses the closest group reported.
- **Some sources are abstracts.** The 2025 multimorbidity result is a conference abstract, not a full paper.
