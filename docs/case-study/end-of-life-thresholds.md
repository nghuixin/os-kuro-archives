# When is it time? Kuro against two end-of-life checklists

Owners of a seriously ill old dog are usually told to watch their dog's quality of life and to talk to the vet "when bad days outnumber good days". This page takes two such tools, the HHHHHMM Quality of Life Scale and the palliative-care guide from Kuro's own vet, puts them side by side, and asks what they would have said about Kuro at each stage of his last two and a half years.

**This is not a judgement on any decision.** Kuro was never euthanised; he died suddenly on 18 Oct 2026 (see the [health summary](health-summary.md#post-mortem-examination-19-oct-2026)). The point is to see how these tools behave on a real, well-documented case, so that other owners know what they catch and what they miss.

## The two tools

**FurKids palliative and end-of-life care guide** ([PDF](../owners/FWVC_PalliativeEnd-of-Life-Care-Support-Guide_2025.pdf), Furkids Wellness Veterinary Centre, 2025). A three-page handout with:

- a **quality-of-life checklist** of five needs, each a yes/no question: appetite, pain, mobility, hygiene, interaction;
- a **decision rule**: "If bad days outnumber good days in a week, it may be time to have a conversation with us";
- **emergency signs** to act on at once: laboured breathing or gasping, cluster seizures, collapse or inability to stand, uncontrolled vomiting or pain, sudden rapid deterioration;
- **home-setup tips**: soft bedding in a quiet area, food and water within reach, non-slip mats, clean and dry skin to avoid sores or urine scald, minimal stress;
- what the clinic offers (pain relief, hydration and nutrition support, anti-nausea drugs, mobility aids, hygiene support) and how euthanasia is handled.

**HHHHHMM Quality of Life Scale** (Villalobos), scored for Kuro in [`hhhhhmm_kuro.csv`](../../data/quality_of_life/hhhhhmm_kuro.csv): seven items scored 0–10, total out of 70, with **above 35 acceptable** for continued palliative care. See [Reading the quality-of-life scores](../../README.md#reading-the-quality-of-life-scores).

## How they line up

| HHHHHMM item | FurKids checklist | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hurt** (pain, incl. breathing) | **Pain**: "signs of discomfort, limping, panting, or hiding?" | Both count breathing; FurKids names panting as a pain sign. FurKids also lists laboured breathing as an emergency. |
| **Hunger** | **Appetite**: "still eating voluntarily?" | Equivalent. |
| **Hydration** | *(not in the checklist)* | FurKids covers it only as support offered (IV, syringe) and a home tip (water within reach). |
| **Hygiene** | **Hygiene**: "clean and free of sores or scalding?" | Equivalent; FurKids names urine scald explicitly. |
| **Happiness** | **Interaction**: "responsive to loved ones or enjoying small pleasures?" | Equivalent. |
| **Mobility** | **Mobility**: "get up, walk, or toilet without distress?" | FurKids adds **toileting**; it also lists "inability to stand" as an emergency. |
| **More good days than bad** | **Decision rule**: bad days outnumber good days **in a week** | Same idea; FurKids makes the time window explicit (a week). |
| *(none)* | **Emergency signs** | HHHHHMM has no red-flag list; it scores the typical state, not acute events. |

The two tools measure almost the same things. The differences that matter: FurKids drops **hydration**, adds **toileting** and an **emergency list**, and gives yes/no answers rather than a score; HHHHHMM gives a **numeric threshold** but no red flags.

## Kuro, block by block

HHHHHMM scores from his records, confirmed by his owner; the FurKids column applies the guide's yes/no questions to the same evidence ("no" where the HHHHHMM item scored 4 or below).

| Block | HHHHHMM total (>35 acceptable) | Lowest items | FurKids checklist "no" | Bad days outnumber good? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 H1 | 60 | — | none | no |
| 2024 H2 | 47 | hurt 6, hydration 6, hygiene 6 | none | no (but the late-June crisis was days of emergency signs) |
| 2025 H1 | 46 | hurt 5, mobility 6, hygiene 6 | none | no |
| 2025 H2 | 51 | hurt 6 | none | no |
| 2026 H1 | 39 | mobility 4, hygiene 4, hydration 5 | **mobility, hygiene** | no |
| 2026 H2 | 36 | mobility 3, hygiene 3, hydration 5, more good days 5 | **mobility, hygiene** | no: "almost entirely good days" in his last two weeks (owner, End of Life survey) |

By either tool's main rule, **Kuro never reached the point of an end-of-life conversation**: his total stayed above 35, and good days never clearly outnumbered by bad. His owner's End of Life answers agree: quality of life 6 out of 7 in his final two weeks.

## What the tools would have flagged

**1. The emergency signs did occur, more than once.** Each was an "act now" event under the FurKids guide:

| Date | Sign | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| late Jun 2024 | laboured breathing, pale gums, collapse of red cell count | ICU, transfusion |
| 15 Apr 2025 | breathing "a bit labored", bloated belly | taken to FurKids the same day |
| 19 Apr 2026 | could not get up after pooping | FurKids visit 20 Apr |
| 9–10 Jun 2026 | **unable to stand**, found lying in urine | seen at acupuncture the same day; toileting help four times a day afterwards |
| 18 Oct 2026 | sudden rapid deterioration (presumed) | found in the pond; cause undetermined |

The records show each of the first four was acted on within about a day. The last could not be: no one saw it happen.

**2. The totals hid two failing needs.** In 2026 his **mobility** and **hygiene** fell to 3–4 out of 10, a "no" on two of FurKids' five questions, yet his total stayed above 35 because **hunger and happiness stayed high**: he was food-driven and engaged to the end. A total can stay "acceptable" while specific needs fail, especially in a dog whose appetite and mood hold up. Watching the **lowest items**, not just the total, would have shown the decline earlier and more plainly.

**3. The weekly good-day/bad-day rule needs a diary that wasn't kept.** The guide's rule is weekly; the records describe six-month blocks and scattered good and bad days. No one counted good and bad days, so the rule could only be applied from memory, after the fact.

**4. Hydration slipped without being on the checklist.** His drinking needed encouragement throughout 2026 (a water chart every 2 hours, an apple in the bowl, urea rising to 17.2). HHHHHMM scored it 5; the FurKids checklist does not ask.

## The home-setup tips, against what was done

| FurKids tip | Kuro's care |
|---|---|
| Soft bedding in a quiet area | slept indoors, in a different spot by day |
| Food and water within reach | water kept within reach and on a 2-hourly chart (from May–Jun 2026) |
| Non-slip mats for stability | mats from 2024 and dog shoes; anti-slip paw grips in 2026; the mats had worn out by his final months (owner's recollection) |
| Clean, dry skin to avoid sores or urine scald | diapers, wipes, blow-drying; urine-scald risk noted by the vet (Jul 2026); toileting help four times a day |
| Minimise stress | quiet home routine; acupuncture and heat packs for comfort |

One environmental risk is on no checklist: **the garden fish pond**. He had slipped near it before (2022), stepped through the softening mat over it a year before he died (Oct 2025), and died in it. For a dog with weak hind legs and poor vision, open water is a hazard in its own right.

## Lessons for other owners

- **Score regularly, at the time.** Both tools work best scored weekly by the people doing the care. Scoring after the fact, as here, is a review, not a decision aid.
- **Watch the lowest item, not only the total.** A total above 35 can hide a dog who can no longer get up or stay clean.
- **Keep a good-day/bad-day tally.** The FurKids rule is simple, but only if someone counts.
- **Treat the emergency list as separate from the score.** Kuro's crises came as acute events between otherwise acceptable scores.
- **Add hydration and hazards** (water, stairs, slippery floors) to whatever checklist you use.
