# Kuro — health summary, 2021 to Oct 2026

Kuro was a male, neutered mixed-breed dog (Embark: 52% Golden Retriever, 34% Japanese Spitz; the family described him as an Australian Shepherd × golden retriever), born on 10 March 2010 in Malaysia. He died on 18 October 2026, aged 16 years 7 months 8 days. His regular vet was FurKids (Furkids Wellness Veterinary Centre); he also had acupuncture at Asia Paws and house calls from Petcare.

This summary is assembled from in-house IDEXX lab reports, 27 vet invoices, medicine labels, feeding notes, clinic images and WhatsApp messages. It tells his story first, then gives the post-mortem findings and the numbers behind them. It records what the reports and vets said; it is not a diagnosis. Data: [`data/processed/`](../../data/processed/).

## His story

### Before the illness (2021–2023)

In his early senior years Kuro's problems were the ordinary ones. He was overweight in January 2021 (31 kg, against a vet's ideal of 26 kg) and slimmed down on raw food, then a cooked-food subscription; by October 2022 the family thought him "very thin". He had eye drops from 2021, dandruff and itchy paws, and in 2022 a fatty lump on his leg was removed. Through 2023 a skin infection with scabbing and itching was kept in check with medicated shampoo, his ears were cleaned with medicated drops, and cataracts were noticed in June ("His eyes got catharac"), later confirmed by a vet. A "low red blood count" was mentioned in May 2023, the first hint of what was to come. At his first FurKids visit in September 2023, "his blood tests are all ok".

### The 2024 crisis

In early 2024 a nutritionist's senior-diet proposal cited weight loss and "high SDMA" (no value is in the records, and every later SDMA was normal), and in March Khan & Kara Enterprise (The RAW Proof) analysed a recipe for him and supplied a tailored raw diet, which turned out to be high in fat.

At the end of June 2024 he became acutely ill: he had vomited, eaten little, limped and had a bloated belly. His blood count collapsed, HCT falling from 34% (27 May) to 24.8% (28 Jun) and 13.2% (30 Jun), and his lipase showed pancreatitis. The vet rated the bloods "critical" and suspected "a mass cancer in the abdomen". He spent a week in intensive care and was given a blood transfusion from a German Shepherd donor. A rescan before planned surgery found an "inoperable mass" in his spleen that had "spread to kidney and some parts of intestine" (a scan impression that the post mortem did not bear out: kidneys and intestines were normal, and no tissue test ever showed the mass was malignant); surgery was cancelled in favour of "palliative care", and the family was told he "can live maybe 3 months, 6 months to a year". He went home on about 5 July on a low-fat diet, prednisolone and iron, and with strict instructions to avoid falls that might rupture the spleen.

### Living with an inoperable tumour (Aug 2024 – 2025)

He outlived that prognosis by more than two years. By August 2024 the pancreatitis had settled ("CPLi back to normal"), and from October darbepoetin injections helped his red cell count. A dangerous dip came in November 2024 (HCT 18.9%, immune-mediated anaemia suspected), but he recovered. The tumour grew slowly, "about 2 cm" by January 2025, and measured 197.5 × 179.7 mm on an x-ray in December 2025.

Through 2025 he had roughly monthly blood tests. His anaemia stayed mild, his platelets ran high and his liver enzymes rose from May; a second pancreatitis flare came in November (lipase 4,451). Meanwhile his back end was ageing. Arthritis ("crepitus") had been found in July 2024, and weekly acupuncture began at Asia Paws. In April 2025 a house-call vet came for "limping and trouble getting up" and found "arthritis and some nerve pain"; from then on he had a warm pack on his hips almost every evening, 321 recorded sessions to April 2026.

### The last year (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026)

In his final year the story shifts from blood counts to mobility and care. His weight, steady at about 22 kg until February 2026, slipped to 19.4 kg by August; the vet called 19.5 kg "the lower limit of his ideal body weight", and his meals were increased twice. His urea climbed (17.2 mmol/L in August) while creatinine and SDMA stayed normal, and a kidney supplement was added.

His hind legs were the main problem. In April 2026 he "couldn't get up" after pooping, and on 9–10 June he could not stand at all and was found lying in his urine, the worst point of his life outside the 2024 crisis. From then on the helper lifted him every hour, he wore diapers, and urine scald became a risk. In July the vet named the cause of the weakness as "sarcopenia (muscle atrophy)".

Yet his last weeks were mostly good. At his final FurKids check on 14 September 2026 his weight had gone up to 19.7 kg, his anaemia was mild (HCT 34.9%) and his tick-borne and heartworm test was negative. At acupuncture in early October the vet found him "clear" apart from "progressive hind limb weakness" and "crampy" muscles, and told the family to "continue making his life heaven on earth". On 9 October she said he was "doing much better", getting up on his own a bit more easily. He was groomed at home on 13 October.

### His death (18 Oct 2026)

On 18 October he was looking for titbits near the terrace in the afternoon. The family noticed he was missing at about 4:30 pm and searched the house, garden and neighbourhood. At 6:48 pm he was found in the small garden fish pond, a few feet from where his father had been. No one saw him go into the water. In the last videos of him, taken between 30 minutes and 2 hours before he died, he was panting heavily, though his family says no more heavily than usual, and did not look particularly energetic. He was cremated on 21 October.

## Post-mortem examination (19 Oct 2026)

FurKids carried out a gross post-mortem examination the day after he died and shared the findings in a message; no formal report was issued. The vet later answered three follow-up questions from the owner (21 Oct).

### Findings

- **Spleen:** "a large tumour in the spleen, with evidence of partial splenic rupture". The rupture "did not appear to be a fresh, acute event", although its age could not be determined from gross examination.
- **Abdomen:** some free blood, "not significant" in amount, and no blood clots. Liver "grossly normal"; kidneys, bladder and intestines without significant abnormalities. Gas in the stomach and intestines was put down to normal post-mortem change. (The 2024 scan had reported spread to the kidney and intestine; the post mortem found these organs normal.)
- **Chest:** "pulmonary congestion with possible focal haemorrhage" in the lungs, but no severe, widespread pulmonary oedema, no fluid in the chest cavity and no fluid or froth in the visible airways. The heart showed no significant abnormality.
- **Nose:** dark red blood from a nostril.

### Cause of death

The vet's stated cause was "**sudden death in a geriatric dog with significant underlying splenic disease, potentially involving acute circulatory or cardiopulmonary compromise.** The exact cause and terminal mechanism remain undetermined." The lung findings made **drowning "less strongly supported"**, and the vet could not tell whether Kuro collapsed before entering the pond, drowned after entering it, or had another terminal event. They suggested the tumour and his general decline "may have contributed", and that weak or unwell dogs sometimes seek a quiet, secluded place to rest. The post-mortem form completed by the family records "Drowned in the fish pond".

### The owner's questions

1. **Was he bleeding internally from the tumour, and could that have been fatal?** There was some free blood in the abdomen but not a significant amount, and no clots. The rupture was not fresh. A large, sudden abdominal haemorrhage is therefore **unlikely** to have been the immediate cause of death.
2. **Where did the blood from his nostril come from?** Bloody nasal discharge can be a post-mortem change, reported in about half of cases. Given the congested lungs, the vet thought the lungs the more likely source, but congestion alone cannot confirm it, and nothing showed that lung bleeding caused his death.
3. **Could smaller, repeated bleeds have affected him before he died?** A post mortem cannot measure blood lost over time: slow or intermittent bleeding may leave little free blood behind. If it had happened, it could have contributed to anaemia, lethargy, reduced exercise tolerance or panting. Without evidence of significant anaemia or other specific findings, the vet could not say how much it contributed.

### What the records add

The records fit the vet's caution. His anaemia was chronic but mild in his last year (HCT 33–35% from July to September 2026, after "No anemia" in February), not the severe drop of June 2024 (13.2%) that marked his known major bleed. His heavy panting in his last videos was, his family says, no heavier than usual, and his father's later mention of "difficulty in breathing" gave no time; neither points clearly to a slower bleed or a sudden circulatory event. What happened in the pond remains unknown. A photograph of the splenic mass taken at the post mortem is held by the clinic.

## Daily care

Much of Kuro's care happened at home, recorded in the household helper's photo log (`home_care.csv`, `stool_log.csv`; Malaysia time).

**Heat therapy.** A hot/cold gel pack was applied to his hindquarters, rump and hips in **321 sessions from May 2025 to Apr 2026**: about twice a day in Jun 2025, then **almost every evening, 7:30–10:30 pm**, through Apr 2026. It began days after the Apr 2025 house call that found "arthritis and some nerve pain". The chats only record warm packs being *recommended* (Oct 2024, Mar 2025, Jul 2025); the photos show it was done.

**Stools.** 63 stool photos, Jul 2024 – May 2025, were scored on the Purina Fecal Scoring Chart (1–7) by two independent raters (exact agreement 69%, never more than one point apart; weighted kappa 0.61 quadratic, 0.51 linear; disagreements settled by a third review).

- **Scores:** 1 (×1), 2 (×41, ideal), 3 (×16), not assessable (×5); nothing softer than 3, and no blood or mucus seen.
- **Over time:** softest in **Jul 2024**, just after the hospital stay, pancreatitis and the switch to a low-fat diet (13 of 26 scored 3; mean 2.5). By **Aug 2024** they had firmed up (17 of 18 scored 2; mean 2.1) and stayed at 2–3 after that. Photos are sparse after Aug 2024 and stop after May 2025.
- **Shape:** all segmented logs, pieces or single logs; none flattened or ribbon-like. Width couldn't be measured (no size reference), and 13 had been picked up before being photographed.
- **Dark stools:** very dark or black pieces on 24 Sep 2024 and 20–21 Nov 2024. They were formed, which makes digested blood less likely, and he was taking activated charcoal, which blackens stool; but the Nov 2024 stools came days after his worst anaemia (HCT 18.9%), so gut bleeding can't be excluded.

**Other observations:** a hind-leg skin lesion (Aug 2024, Apr 2025; e-collar from May 2025), and a diaper or belly wrap and stained bedding in Feb–Mar 2026.

**Grooming** (`grooming.csv`, `providers.csv`): Groomingbygracie until 2023, with occasional sessions at Pawsrepublic; then Gooddoggrooming at home every 2–3 weeks from Jan 2024 to his last session on 13 Oct 2026 (49 sessions), moved indoors after the 2024 hospital stay to avoid falls. The groomer's notes track his skin: tail hair loss (Apr–May 2024), dandruff (Jun 2024), "the skin improve a lot" (Sep 2024), a crusty tail and body odour (Oct 2024), dirty ears (May 2025). In his last year there were 13 sessions, with gaps in Apr and Aug–Sep 2026.

**Dental care.** Teeth cleaned by the grooming service in Jan 2024; a cleaning planned for Jul 2024 was postponed because he was too weak, and one offered for Aug 2025 was put on hold. A professional cleaning in Jan 2026 is recalled by the owner but not recorded. Home brushing was occasional.

**Animal communicator (non-clinical).** From Jul 2024 to Sep 2026 an animal communicator, Petsactuallycantalk, gave six communication sessions and 43 remote reiki sessions (`communicator_notes.csv`). These are intuitive impressions, not examinations, but they track the clinical picture:

- **Abdominal pain** after the Jul 2024 transfusion ("up to 8/10"), with a tender belly again in May 2025.
- **Hips, spine and joints** in nearly every session: stiff walking (Jan 2025), hip discomfort "at times" but better (Aug 2025), and aches worse on cold, rainy nights (Oct 2025 – Mar 2026).
- **Throat and breathing:** a "stuck" or phlegmy throat (2024–25, when the owner also noticed coughing), and wheezy or "asthma"-like panting from Oct 2025, when the owner noticed more panting at night.
- **Other recurring areas:** dry eyes, gums and bad breath, itchy ears and skin, kidney and bladder.
- **Left rib cage and stomach** in Jul 2026; the post mortem later found the splenic tumour on that side.
- **How he was described:** "as healthy as he can be for his age" (Aug 2025) and "I'm as healthy as I can be" (Sep 2026).
